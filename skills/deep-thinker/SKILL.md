# Deep Thinker

A well-framed problem is already half-solved. Most expensive failures come from solving the wrong problem well, not solving the right one badly. This skill is about framing before answering, not about answering fast.

## Principles

1. **Clarify before acting** — ask 2-3 targeted questions first.
2. **Think in systems** — look for connections, not isolated parts.
3. **One framework at a time** — finish applying each before switching.
4. **End with action** — always land on a concrete next step, not just analysis.

## Step 1: Clarify

Establish **Who / What / Why** in 2-3 questions total:

| Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Who | Who's affected? Who decides? |
| What | What does success look like, and how is it measured? |
| Why | Why now? What happens if nothing changes? |

## Step 2: Apply frameworks in parallel

Pick 2-3 complementary mental models based on problem type, and look for where their outputs reinforce or contradict each other:

| Problem type | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Root cause unclear | Five-whys, `systematic-debugging` |
| Needs structured decomposition | `graph-thinking`, hypothesis-tree |
| User motivation unclear | Jobs-to-be-done |
| System of interacting parts | `graph-thinking` |
| Strategic/high-stakes | `tree-of-thoughts`, `self-consistency` |
| Multiple plausible solutions | `tree-of-thoughts` |

Cross-pollinate: does one framework's output contradict another's? That tension is usually the most interesting thing you'll find.

## Step 3: Validate before finalizing

- What are we assuming?
- What's the cost of being wrong?
- What would change our mind?

## Output format

```
## Problem: [Name]

### Clarity
- Who: [stakeholders + decision-makers]
- What: [problem + success criteria]
- Why: [motivation + urgency]

### Approach
[Framework(s) used] — [why they apply]

### Next Action
[Specific first step]
```
