# Historical Research — Axes, Probes, and Section Template

Use this file for `type == historical` research: events, eras, causation, historiography, evolution of ideas or institutions. Load after the user confirms scope.

Probe the user: "What period or event? Are you looking for a narrative, a causal analysis, a historiographical review, or lessons applicable to today?"

## Step 2 axes — Historical landscape

| Agent | Axis | Key questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Timeline and narrative** | Key events in chronological order — what happened, when, who was involved, what were the immediate consequences |
| 2 | **Context and conditions** | What were the social, economic, political, and technological conditions that made this possible or inevitable |
| 3 | **Key actors** | Who were the decision-makers, influencers, and affected parties? What motivated them? |
| 4 | **Primary sources** | What contemporary accounts, documents, data, or artifacts survive? How reliable are they? |

## Step 3 axes — Causation and interpretation

| Agent | Axis | Key questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Causal analysis** | Why did this happen? Immediate causes vs underlying conditions vs trigger events. Competing causal theories |
| 2 | **Historiographical debates** | How have historians interpreted this differently? What are the major schools of thought? What evidence supports each? |
| 3 | **Counterfactuals** | What could have gone differently? What were the realistic alternative paths? |

## Step 4 axes — Legacy and lessons

| Agent | Axis | Key questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Consequences and legacy** | What changed as a result? Short-term vs long-term consequences. How does this event/era shape the present? |
| 2 | **Lessons and analogies** | What lessons does this hold for the present? What are valid and invalid analogies to current situations? |
| 3 | **Unresolved questions** | What do historians still debate? What evidence is missing? What might change the interpretation? |
